## The Planetary Engine

Earth's rigid outer lithosphere is broken into approximately eight major tectonic plates and numerous microplates. These plates move relative to one another at speeds ranging from **1 to 15 centimeters per year**—roughly the speed that human fingernails grow.

### What Drives Plate Motion?

For decades after Alfred Wegener first proposed Continental Drift in 1912, scientists lacked a viable physical mechanism. The discovery of mid-ocean ridges, paleomagnetic zebra-striping on the seafloor, and deep-focus earthquakes in the 1960s revealed the driving forces:

1. **Slab Pull (Primary Force):** Cold oceanic lithosphere is denser than the warm asthenospheric mantle beneath it. Gravity pulls the sinking slab downward into the subduction trench, dragging the rest of the trailing plate across the globe like a heavy tablecloth sliding off a table.
2. **Ridge Push (Secondary Force):** Mid-ocean ridges stand 2 to 3 km higher than the surrounding abyssal plains because hot, newly upwelled mantle rock is thermally expanded and buoyant. Gravity causes the elevated ridge flanks to slide outward, pushing adjacent lithosphere away from the spreading axis.
3. **Mantle Drag / Basal Tractions:** Convective currents within the ductile asthenosphere exert shear stresses along the base of tectonic plates.
