## What is Stratigraphy?

Stratigraphy is the study of rock layers (strata) and layering (stratification). Because sedimentary rocks form from sediments deposited by gravity in water or wind, they accumulate in sequential horizontal sheets.

### Steno's Principles of Relative Dating

1. **Law of Superposition:** In an undeformed sequence of sedimentary rocks, the oldest layer is at the bottom, and layers become progressively younger toward the top.
2. **Principle of Original Horizontality:** Sedimentary layers are initially deposited essentially flat under the influence of gravity. Tilted, folded, or faulted layers were deformed by tectonic forces after deposition.
3. **Principle of Lateral Continuity:** Sedimentary strata extend laterally in all directions until they thin out or encounter a barrier. If a river carves a canyon, matching strata on opposite canyon walls can be correlated across the gap.
4. **Principle of Cross-Cutting Relationships:** Any geological feature (such as a fault or igneous intrusion) that cuts across or deforms another rock unit must be younger than the unit it disrupts.
