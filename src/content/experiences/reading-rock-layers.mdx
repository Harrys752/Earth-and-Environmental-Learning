## Beat 1: The Pages of Earth's History Book

When you drive through a mountain pass or walk along a rocky coastline, you often see tall cliffs striped with parallel bands of colored rock: red sandstone, gray shale, and white limestone stacked one on top of another.

To untrained eyes, these are just colorful stones. To an Earth scientist, **each layer is a preserved chapter of planetary history**.

A layer of sandstone tells of an ancient desert of sand dunes or a tropical beach. A layer of black shale tells of an ancient quiet lake or oxygen-depleted ocean basin. A layer of limestone filled with shell fragments tells of a warm, sunlit coral reef.

---

## Beat 2: Nicolaus Steno and the Rules of Relative Dating

In the 17th century, Danish polymath **Nicolaus Steno** realized that sedimentary rocks follow fundamental physical laws governing how particles settle under gravity:

1. **Law of Superposition:** In an undisturbed sequence of sedimentary rocks, the oldest layer is at the bottom, and each overlying layer is progressively younger.
2. **Principle of Original Horizontality:** Sedimentary layers settle out of water or air as horizontal sheets. If you see rock layers tilted at a 45-degree angle or bent into sharp folds, **tectonic forces buckled them after they formed**.
3. **Principle of Cross-Cutting Relationships:** If an igneous intrusion (such as a volcanic magma dike) or a fault cuts across rock layers, **the intrusion or fault must be younger than the rocks it cuts through**.

Using these three simple yet powerful rules, geologists can determine the chronological sequence of geological events without needing radioactive dating equipment.

## Beat 3: Unconformities — Missing Pages in Time

What happens when rock layers are uplifted above sea level and exposed to rain, rivers, and wind? They erode away.

When the land later sinks back beneath the ocean and new layers are deposited on top of the eroded surface, a gap in the geological record is preserved. This boundary is called an **unconformity**.

An unconformity represents a missing interval of time—sometimes tens or hundreds of millions of years of Earth history that was stripped away by erosion before new sediments buried the ancient landscape.
