## Beat 1: The Opening Question

> **A single grain of quartz or volcanic sand on a Javanese riverbank might once have erupted from a fiery stratovolcano, and might millions of years from now become part of a towering mountain range. How does Earth continually reshape its solid crust?**

To human eyes, rocks seem eternal and unchanging. Yet the ground beneath our feet is participating in an unending, slow-motion planetary recycling system: the **rock cycle**.

Driven by two grand thermal engines—Earth's internal radioactive heat engine and the external radiant energy of the Sun—every rock on our planet is constantly formed, broken down, deformed, and reborn.

---

## Beat 2: The Three Rock Classes

Geologists classify all crustal rocks into three fundamental families based on their genetic origin:

1. **Igneous Rocks (Fire-Formed):**
   - Born when molten magma (underground) or lava (at the surface) cools and crystallizes.
   - *Intrusive (Plutonic):* Cooled slowly deep in the crust, producing coarse, visible interlocking crystals (e.g., Granite, Diorite).
   - *Extrusive (Volcanic):* Cooled rapidly at the surface, producing fine-grained or glassy textures (e.g., Basalt, Andesite, Obsidian).

2. **Sedimentary Rocks (Layered & Lithified):**
   - Formed at Earth's surface when pre-existing rocks are broken down into sediment by weathering, transported by water or wind, deposited in basins, and compacted/cemented through **lithification** (e.g., Sandstone, Shale, Limestone).

3. **Metamorphic Rocks (Transformed by Heat & Pressure):**
   - Formed in the solid state when existing rocks are subjected to intense temperature and confining/differential pressure without fully melting. Mineral grains recrystallize into dense, often banded (foliated) textures (e.g., Marble from limestone, Schist, Gneiss).

## Beat 3: The Transformative Geological Pathways

The rock cycle is not a simple, one-way circular track. It is an interconnected web where any rock family can transform into any other:

```
                  [ Magma / Lava ]
                     /         \
       Cooling &    /           \ Melting
    Crystallization/             \
                  ↓               \
          [ Igneous Rock ]         \
             /          \           \
  Weathering/            \ Heat &    \
& Erosion  /              \ Pressure  \
          ↓                ↓           \
    [ Sediment ]     [ Metamorphic ]    \
          |           [    Rock     ] ←--+
Lithification         /          ↑
 (Compaction)        / Heat &    | Heat &
          ↓         /  Pressure  | Pressure
   [ Sedimentary ]-/             |
   [    Rock     ]---------------+
```

- **Weathering & Erosion:** Physical freeze-thaw/thermal expansion and chemical reactions with rain break surface rock into mineral grains and ions.
- **Lithification:** Burial pressure compacts sediment, while mineral-bearing groundwater precipitates calcite or quartz "glue," welding particles into stone.
- **Metamorphism:** Tectonic collision or deep burial heats stone up to 300°C–800°C, causing solid-state recrystallization.
- **Melting:** When temperatures exceed the melting curve, rock returns to liquid magma, resetting the cycle.
