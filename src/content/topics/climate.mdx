## The Planetary Heat Engine

Climate is the long-term statistical pattern of weather over decades, centuries, and millennia. It is governed fundamentally by a global thermodynamic balance: **incoming solar radiation vs. outgoing terrestrial heat loss**.

Because the Earth is a sphere, the equator receives direct, concentrated sunlight year-round, while polar regions receive sunlight at steep, glancing angles spread over much larger surface areas.

### Global Climate Dynamics
- **Uneven Solar Heating:** High energy density in the tropics generates buoyant, rising equatorial air masses.
- **Global Circulation Cells:** Hadley, Ferrel, and Polar atmospheric cells redistribute excess equatorial heat toward the cold poles.
- **Tropical Monsoons:** Large-scale seasonal wind reversals driven by the differential heat capacities of landmasses and surrounding oceans (such as the Asian-Australian Monsoon governing Indonesia).
