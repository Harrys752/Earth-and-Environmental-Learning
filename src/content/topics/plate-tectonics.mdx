## Understanding Earth's Dynamic Shell

Unlike the static surfaces of the Moon or Mars, Earth is geologically alive. The planet's heat engine—driven by primordial heat leftover from planetary accretion and the continuous radioactive decay of isotopes like Uranium, Thorium, and Potassium—creates convective currents in the mantle.

These currents stress and fracture the brittle lithosphere into a mosaic of tectonic plates. Where these plates grind together, pull apart, or plunge into the depths, the most dramatic geological phenomena on Earth unfold:

### Key Geological Boundaries
1. **Convergent Boundaries (Subduction & Collision):** Where plates collide, driving deep-sea trenches, mountain belts, and explosive volcanic arcs.
2. **Divergent Boundaries (Rifting & Spreading):** Where plates separate, creating rift valleys on land and mid-ocean ridges beneath the seas.
3. **Transform Boundaries (Shear):** Where plates slide horizontally past each other, accumulating immense elastic strain that releases in violent earthquakes.
