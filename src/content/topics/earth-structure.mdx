## Journey into the Planetary Interior

We inhabit the outermost skin of a dynamic, heat-driven sphere. While human exploration has physically reached only 12.2 km beneath the surface, geophysical methods—primarily the propagation of seismic shockwaves from earthquakes—allow scientists to map Earth's interior all the way to its center 6,371 km below.

By analyzing how P-waves and S-waves refract, reflect, and cast shadow zones across the globe, geophysicists decipher:
- The brittle, rigid **lithospheric crust** that floats atop the convective mantle.
- The high-temperature, ductile **silicate mantle** driving global continental drift.
- The circulating liquid iron-nickel **outer core** that generates Earth's geomagnetic protective shield.
- The ultra-dense, solid crystalline **inner core** enduring temperatures exceeding 5,000°C.
